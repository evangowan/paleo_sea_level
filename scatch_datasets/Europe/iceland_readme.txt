NorddahlPetursson2005 -  The paper itself has new radiocarbon dates. Some do not have the details on elevation, so they could not be included. I took the location from Google Earth based on the location names. The radiocarbon dates from Auðbjargarstaðir, Rauðinúpur, and Tjarnargata do not have any elevation information, so they are not included. Tjarnargata

NorddahlSaemundsson1999 - The Rauðamelur sedimentary sequence.  From figure 3.3 in Norddahl and Petursson 2005, this section has two spit deposits that are from before and after that last glaciation of iceland. I ma the age of the MIS 3 whalebone at face value. The details of the elevation measurements are vague, except to say the outcrop is 20-25 m.  taken from a spit that is overlain by a lava flow that was subaerially erupted, and late Weichselian glaciation. Based on the description, this sand and gravel deposit is likely a beach or beach ridge deposit. I am taking this range as the elevation in both the pre and post glacial deposits, and considering it as a beach indicator. The location is taken from Google Earth based on the map in Norddahl and Petursson 2005. The range is taken from IMCalc.

JohannessonEtal1997 - This is another conference abstract, with several radiocarbon dates from four locations on the Reykjanes Peninsula. One includes a sample from the same site described in NorddahlSaemundsson1999 at the Rauðamelur sedimentary sequence. Six samples were from Njarðvíkurheiði, all of LGM age.  The description of this site is in JohannesonSaemundsson1995. The shells are in silt and sandstone. Some shells are regarded as being in-situ, but the indicative meaning is not clear, so I have set this to be marine limiting. They state that this from a marine terrace originally at ~19 m, but I am not confident enough to assign as a sea level indicator. No details on the elevation measurement are reported, so the uncertainty is 20% of the reported elevation of 19 m. The location is estimated from Google Earth by the map in NorddahlPetursson2005. The sample from Sandgerði (AAR-2803) does not have any information on its elevation, so it cannot be included.

EirikssonEtal1997 - Contains a description of three sections on the tip of the Seltjarnarnes penninsula just west of Reykjavik. I am assuming that the elevations (based on figure 5 in the paper) are relative to mean sea level. Without further details, I have applied an uncertainty of 1 m. The location was estimated from Google Earth from the map in the paper. The site labeled A has two samples from unit 2, which is a diamictite with shells that were regarded as being in living position. Assuming that this is not deformed, this can still only be regarded as marine limiting. The sample Ua-2653 is from unit 4 of the same outcrop. This unit is described as a shelly sand, but the presence of mud indicates meant they interpreted it as being sublitoral, and therefore we must set it to be marine limiting. AAR-2507 is a sample taken from the section labeled B, from unit 2. This unit was described as having low angle beded gravel and coarse sand, which they as being shallow marine, with the possibility that it is a beach or shoreface deposit. I used IMCalc to calculate the indicator as a beach deposit. There are three other radiocarbon dates reported, but they are from a diamiction that has obvious signs of deformation, and therefore is not a reliable indicator.

Andrews et al 2000 - The dates are discussed in NorddahlPetursson2005, but these samples are all from sediment cores taken from the Iceland shelf and slope at depths >-94 m, and therefore are not relevant to sea level.

Eiríksson et al 2000 - There is also a date from this paper discussed in NorddahlPetursson2005, but this is also a deep ocean sediment study not relevant to sea level.

LeviEtal1990 - This paper reports on subaerially erupted lava from the Reykjanes Penninsula that was erupted during the Laschamp excursion, which they date with the Ar/Ar method averaging 42.9 ± 7.8. They do not state specific cite elevation of individual samples, but state the lavas are at elevations between 80 and 150 m. The hills that contain these lavas were interpreted to have formed when the ice thickness did not exceed the height of the subaerially erupted lava (i.e. the hill protruded out of glacier). I am taking the lower end and making it a terrestrial indicator with an uncertainty of 10 m, as even with the glacial cover, the GIA response was not sufficient to submerge this area.

Ashwell1975 - reports three radicarbon dated glaciomarine sediment samples. This environment can only be considered marine limiting. The locations of the samples are estimated on Google Earth based on a site map. The radiocarbon dates need to be corrected for fractionation. It seems likely that the radiocarbon dates were from a mixture of shell species. The table with the samples lists the elevation as "height of shell layers" with an elevation range. It is unclear if this was the estimated elevation of the samples, or if this was the thickness of the unit. I am taking the halfway point of the given range, and using half the range as the uncertainty or 10 m (over 50 m)/20% of the elevation, whichever is larger.

Petursson1991 - Pétursson, 1986, 1988, 1991 - I am hoping all the information is within the summary paper Pétursson, 1991, because the other two are not accessable. The work is based in Melrakkasletta, which is in northeastern Iceland. Pétursson notes there has been tectonic subsidence since deglaciation, but the general trends in paleo sea level show that there is a dominant GIA signal. Locations are estimated on Google Earth using the site map. There are a number of radiocarbon dates from Trondheim which have a 440 year correction applied to them. To bring it back to the standard -25 permille, I added the 440 years back to the dates. The site at Hvalvík, unit 9, doesn't give a specific elevation where the shells were collected, but says the deposit (glaciomarine sediments) formed when sea level was above 40 m, so this is what I use to make it a marine limiting point. The shells are Mya truncata and Hiatella arctica, so no deposit feeder impacts. The second sample from Hvalvík is from unit 10, which is a coarsening upwards sand to coarse sand and gravel. Pétursson says this unit formed when sea level was at least 30 m above present, but the sample comes from the contact between the lower unit 9 and 10, which is below that. I am using this 30 m value as a marine limiting point. The sample T-4469 is from a deformed till, so it is not useful as a sea level indicator and is not included. The sample from Kópasker is described as a sublittoral gravel below the marine limit at 5-6 m. The lack of description on the precise elevation and indicative meaning means it can only be considered as marine limiting. I am taking the 5 m, and applying a 1 m uncertainty. The Uppsala date is also described in OlsonEtal1972. It is in marine clay, so is marine limiting. The exact elevation of the dated unit is not stated, but based on the diagram (figure 8), it has to be less than about 6 m. I am using 6 m as the elevation, with a 1.2 m uncertainty.

MagnusdottirNorddahl2000 - The locations of the samples are estimated on Google Earth based on a site map. This has two original dates. One date (AAR-3735) is of a whale bone (ribs), which is at 80m above sea level, in a gravel spit. They suggest that this bone represents the age of the marine limit, which is at 105 m. However, I am being conservative and using the elevation of the fossil and setting it to be marine limiting. The second sample (AAR-3654) is from a gravel pit that was interpreted as a raised shoreline at 55 m. I have set this as a index point, with an interpretation of it being a beach.

IngolfssonNorddahl2001 -  This has two unique dates from Stóri-Sandhóll. The samples come from a glaciomarine diamiction, and can only be considered as marine limiting. The elevation was determined by a repeated barometer measurements using a benchmark at the site. They state an uncertainty of 2 m, which I will use. The location is taken from Google Earth based on the site map in the paper.

Ingolfsson1987 - More details on dates are in Hakansson1984 and Hakansson1986, including the locations. The elevation measurement techniques are not stated, so I have applied 20% uncertainty for samples above 5 m, and 1 m for anything less than that. The first five samples (Lu-2193 Lu-2192 Lu-2195 Lu-2379 Lu-2377) are from a glaciomarine diamication. These samples can only be regarded as marine limiting, though they say that sea level was likely close to the local marine limit of 80 m. The next four samples (Lu-2196, Lu-2372, Lu-2373, Lu-2376) are from silt and sand deposits, that indicate shallower water (even brackish), though the sea level was still above the elevation of the outcrop (23 m). They state that some of the ages are likely maximum ages for the deposit. Regardless, these are all considered to be marine limiting. The last sample (Lu-2375) is in a silt and sand deposit, which could be a beach deposit. The age was regarded as too old for the deposit, and therefore is likely redeposited. I have excluded this sample.

SveinbjornsdottirJohnsen1990 - This has a list of radiocarbon dates from mollusc shells, but contains no information on elevation or the sediments that the samples are from. I am searching other papers for these details.

AndersenEtal1989 - This paper was not in the NorddahlPetursson2005 compilation, but does have some dates pertaining to late glacial sea level. All samples were regarded as in situ. They state the elevation was about 3 m for Nauthólsvík, while the Skerjafjördur were at 0 m. One other sample at Austurströnd was at 8 m. I am applying a 1 m uncertainty for the first two samples, and 1.6 m for the last. I used the inner fraction dates for the multiply dated samples. The indicative meaning of the sediments is not explained in detail. I am assigning them all to be marine limiting. The locations are taken from Google Earth based on the map in the paper.

SveinbjornsdottirEtal1993 - This paper provides some more details on the samples reported in AndersenEtal1989, as well as many other samples (including some from SveinbjornsdottirJohnsen1990). From their description (siltstone,sandstone with with in situ molluscs and sometimes dropstones), everything can be considered marine limiting. The sample locations were taken from Google Earth based on the locations on the map in the paper. The elevation measurments are not stated, only a thickness of the outcrop. Since the bottom of the outcrop is undoubtedly at least at sea level or higher, the best that can be stated is that the marine limit is higher than the height in the outcrop. I applied a 1 m uncertainty.

Ingolfsson1985 - This contains a number of radiocarbon dates that are from marine sediments. The deposits were interpreted to have been deposited when sea level was as much as 60 m above present, so tens of meters above the elevation of the deposits. These shells therefore are more relevant to deglaciation rather than defining sea level. Nevertheless, I have included them as marine limiting, using the highest elevation of the range presented on the table, and using 20% of the elevation as the uncertainty. More details on the dates are in the Lund radiocarbon date lists, which I use as the reference for the ages (they were reservoir corrected in the Ingolfsson papers). The locations were taken from Google Earth based on the map in the paper if they were not in the Lund lists.

Ingolfsson1988 - As with the other Ingolfsson papers from the 80s, I refer to the Lund radiocarbon lists for the details. The sample labeled Lu-2394 is wrong. It is actually Lu-2396 in the Lund lists. The peat dates are relative to the high tide mark. From Gehrels et al 2006, the tidal range in western Iceland is just over 2 m, so I am subtracting 1 m from these data to make them close to sea level (in addition to the 1 m uncertainty since the real tidal range at the site location is not stated.

Hjartarson1993 - This as a few dates, the location was taken from Google Earth based on the map in the paper. The sample AAR-120 is from a moraine and therefore is not included. The elevations are based on the sections in the paper. The sample AAR-125 was described as being taken 1-2 m above sea level. As with other marine limiting samples, I take the high end of the range and apply a 1 m uncertainty. Samples AAR-117, AAR-118, AAR-926 are from till layers that show signs of deformation, and are not included. AAR-122 is also from a till layer and not included. AAR-121 and AAR-927 are from a marine sediments that are at 12-14 m, according to the text of the article. I put the upper elevation with 20% as the error.

Norddahl1990 - This is a review paper that includes four pers comms radiocarbon dates. Two of the dates (U-2227 and U-2596) are not described in detail, but the information on U-2227 is available in the Uppsala XI date list (OlssonEtal1972). U-2817 and U-2898 come from the bottomset beds of a delta described as being at about 40 m. Only U-2817 can be considered reliable, as U-2898 was collected from shells that were apparently weathered out of the sediments. I set U-2817 to be marine limiting at 40 m with an uncertanty of 20%. Location taken from Google Earth based on the map in the paper.

OlssonEtal1972 - Contains some data for Iceland. U-724 states it is from a silt, which is pretty vague and I have assigned to be marine limiting. U-2227 does not have elevation information.
