Baltic.ods - This is the Rosentau et al 2021 HOLSEA dataset, without any modifications aside from putting it into my format
Vink North Sea - This is the Vink et al dataset, which is in a format comparable with HOLSEA. It seems that I also put in the data from the Scheder et al 2022 Holsea dataset. I moved the data from the Netherlands into the superceded section since there is now the HOLSEA_NL dataset.
Rotterdam - this contains the HOLSEA dataset from Hijma and Cohen 2019 which covers Rotterdam in the Netherlands. Since all this data are in the HOLSEA_NL database, this is no longer included in the database.
Limfjord - There was a gap between the Baltic and North Sea datasets in western Denmark, so I filled it with the data from this paper
Gehrels_Ho_Bugt - There was a gap between the Baltic and North Sea datasets in western Denmark, so I filled it with the data from this paper
Shennan_Sea_level_index_points_2022.ods - Ian Shennan kindly sent me an updated (ca. 2022) version of the British and Irish database, which includes all of the information that is needed to include in the database (the 2018 database lacked the information to calibrate the dates). The main thing here was tracking down all the references to add to the bibtex file. Two samples (HV7094 and HV7095) were already included in the Vink database, so I have placed them into a separate sheet and are not added from this database. The location for the sample GU-910 was incorrect and I fixed it.
Irish_RSL_Database.ods - This is the Brooks and Edwards Irish sea level database, which is no longer online at the link described in the paper. I found this on Researchgate and assume it is complete. This is just for reference, since all of the data in here should also be in the Shennan et al database.
Faroe_Islands.ods - I could only find two studies with useable sea level data for the Faroe Islands, which are in this spreadsheet.
Creel_Norway.ods - This is the HOLSEA formatted dataset for Norway by Creel et al 2022. I found an error on sample ID 618, which had the incorrect age for a stratigraphic based date described originally as "The ingression contact occurs a few cm below the Allerød-YD boundary in the core, as inferred from the pollen record". The Date in the Creel database is only 1000, but based on the description, I have changed this to be 13000+/-100 based on it being below the Younger Dryas boundary, which is about 12900 kyr BP. There are a number of samples from Barnett et all that are from industrial period. For the purposes of the paleo sea level database, I have not included these. The "corrected" radiocarbon dates have a bulk error applied to them in applicable. My preference is to do this during the calibration process, so I have reverted the errors in my own list. The fractionation correction is of course applied, based on what Creel et al did. There seems to be two erroneous lines in the radiocarbon list that do not correspond to the lines from the long-form sheet. I have removed them and it appears that everything is aligned properly after that. I have adjusted the "unique sample ID" field to reflect this. The dates from Bakke et al 2005 were corrected for fractionation, but this is clearly an error since they should have already been corrected. I reverted the ages to what was in the original paper. For calibration of marine samples, I am using Normarine18 for samples that are older than 10900 radiocarbon years BP (the Normarine18 curve starts at 12200 cal yr BP, which is 10827 radiocarbon years BP). To ensure continuity, I appeneded the Marine20 curve with a reservoir correction of -133 ± 54 (as suggested by my collaborators from the DATED project). There are a few dates in this database that are in Russia. I have moved these to a separate spreadsheet in the Eurasian Arctic folder. Sample #1005 (T-520) should not be included. It is from a lake in the mountains, and it seems the elevation was incorrectly entered. I has no relevance to sea level.
Gulf_Of_Riga_Rosentau.ods - Some samples from Estonia and Latvia from Rosentau et al 2023. These do not appear to be in the wider Baltic database.
Brader_2017_Iceland.ods - Isolation basin based SLIPs from northwestern Iceland. These were bulk sediment radiocarbon dates, so I added a 100 year bulk correction.
Brader_2015_Iceland.ods - Isolation basin based SLIPs from western Iceland. These were bulk sediment radiocarbon dates, so I added a 100 year bulk correction. There was no information on the elevation uncertainties, but I assume it was the same as the Brader et al 2017 surveys, which had an uncertainty of 0.3 m. The data from Ytra-Bárvatn were not reliable and not included.
Lloyd_2009_Iceland.ods - Isolation basin based SLIPs from northwestern Iceland.  These were bulk sediment radiocarbon dates, so I added a 100 year bulk correction. There was no elevation uncertainties stated, but I assume it was the same as the Brader et al 2017 surveys, which had an uncertainty of 0.3 m. I removed the samples that did not have an interpreted sea level position.
Atlantic_Coast_Garcia-Artola.ods - Compilation of data from the Atlantic coast of Europe (France, Spain and Portugal). My thanks to Ane García-Artola for sending me the radiocarbon information that was absent in the published dataset. I also included one point from García-Artola et al (2023) which was from Basque Country.
Katrantsiotis_Southeast_Sweden.ods - These data are from isolation basins. No elevation uncertainty is stated. The elevation of the basin sills were determined by Lidar with a stated uncertainty of 0.1 m, so that is what I am using. I am assuming that the "type" is entered incorrectly (it was set to terrestrial limiting), and these are actually all index points.
Grant_Humber.ods - Two index points from the Humber Estuary in eastern England. 
Hijma_Doggerland.ods - This contains some points that were also in the Vink database. These points are superceded by this database. There are four rejected points, which I have not included. Hijma et al applied compaction and vertical land motion. In all cases, the bulk uncertainty (100 years) has been applied to the data. I have split this out so that it can be recalibrated with my own scripts.
Sylt_region.ods - In order to fill a gap in the database, I have added a few dates from Lindhorst et al from Sylt. They are radiocarbon dates of shells from the late Holocene, and are marine limiting. The core with the radiocarbon dates is 7.85 m long, with the bottom at 6 m below sea level, so I assume that 1.85 m can be subtracted to get the depth relative to sea level. The position was estimated the site map using Google Earth.
Bungenstock_Frisian.ods - I am pretty sure I had this dataset in here before, but it did not make the transition to version 2.0, probably by mistake. Some of the data are superceded by the Hijma dataset.
Zhang_Eiderstedt.ods - This contains a bunch of radiocarbon and OSL dates from a core in northwestern Germany. The core shows a transgression at the end of the Pleistocene and a regression in the Late Holocene. The stratigraphy suggest shoreface, offshore and foreshore (tidal flat) deposits. The authors provide no interpretation on the sea level position, so I have conservatively set them to be marine limiting. The details of the elevation says that top of the drill core was "close to modern sea level". I am using a ±1 m uncertainty to everything.
Minch_NW_Scotland.ods - This contains two papers published about paleo sea level in northwestern Scotland, which used OSL dating. Plenty of the OSL dates were considered to be too old, so they are not included. The two MIS 3 dates from Simms et al are from a shore platform. I used the reported elevation and range of the platform (28 m) as the paleo sea level, as that is what they did in the paper. The coordinates were not given, so I estimated it from Google Earth based on the map in the paper.
HOLSEA_NL_extract.ods - This has extracted data from the HOLSEA-NL dataset. The original dataset was too big to deal with. I used the recommended bulk corrections. Note, they have included both indicators of sea level position and groundwater position in the database. The groundwater position points (TYPE=2,3) are not to be used in the sea level database. They include points with and without tectonic correction. This file includes the points without tectonic correction, though compaction and other corrections are still applied.
Iceland.ods - This includes the new compilation of data for Iceland (excluding the data from Brader et al 2017, 2015, and Lloyd et al 2009, which are in separate spreadsheets). To account for marine limit data, I have used the type=-2 code. I used this code because the HOLSEA_NL dataset uses numbers greater than 1 for groundwater level indicators. For more information on this compilation, look at iceland_readme.txt.
